Jensen Huang's most quoted line about AI and jobs is missing a detail.

At the Milken conference in 2025 he said: "You're not going to lose your job to an AI, but you're going to lose your job to someone who use AI."

Stanford's Digital Economy Lab updated its payroll study in August 2026. The study uses ADP payroll data.

Two findings stand out.

Employment for workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed jobs now sits about 19% below trend.

Experienced workers in the same jobs show no comparable decline.

And the gap comes from fewer hires, not more firings.

So for a junior, the competitor isn't a peer who uses AI better.

The competitor is a senior who uses AI and no longer needs a junior.

Nobody gets fired in this story. A job posting never goes up.

My read: AI pressure shows up first on the bottom rung, because junior work runs on codified knowledge. Senior work runs on judgment, context and relationships.

If you're early in your career, "learn AI" won't be enough. Everyone will.

Build proof of judgment. Public work. Decisions you made and why. Problems a senior would otherwise have to check.

If you hire, ask the uncomfortable question: where will your seniors come from in 2032 if nobody hires juniors in 2026?

POST
AI has a positioning problem. The numbers keep getting worse.

Pew, August 2026: 52% of Americans feel more concerned than excited about AI in daily life. In 2021 the figure was 37%.

Among adults under 30, 55% now say concerned over excited. The first time a majority of young adults said so.

73% of under-30s expect AI to mean fewer jobs over the next 20 years. Two years ago: 61%.

Now pair this with a 2024 study from Washington State University and Temple University. Six experiments. Identical product descriptions. One version mentioned "artificial intelligence". The other didn't.

The AI version lowered purchase intention. The drop ran through emotional trust. And the effect grew stronger for high-risk products.

Put together, here's the uncomfortable conclusion for founders and marketers.

The word "AI" on your landing page reads as a feature to investors and a warning label to many customers.

Two audiences. One word. Opposite reactions.

What I'd do:
• Sell the outcome, not the technology. "Answers in 2 minutes" beats "AI-powered support".
• Save the AI story for buyers who evaluate the stack.
• In high-risk categories (health, money, safety), lead with human accountability.

My hypothesis: the best AI products of the next few years will barely mention AI.

Microsoft studied why AI isn't paying off inside companies. The answer wasn't the AI.

In the 2026 Work Trend Index, about 67% of the real impact of AI traced back to organisational factors. Culture. Manager support. Talent practices.

Only 32% came from individual capability.

Microsoft calls the gap the Transformation Paradox. People are ready. Organisations are not.

Now line up a 2025 report from MIT's Project NANDA. After $30 to 40 billion of enterprise spending, 95% of organisations reported no measurable return from generative AI.

Most people read the 95% as "AI doesn't work."

I read both reports as the same diagnosis.

AI works at the level of a person. Returns show up at the level of an organisation. And most organisations still run on workflows designed before AI.

A licence changes what one person produces. A workflow change decides whether the company captures the gain.

The practical test for any leader:
• Pick one process.
• Redesign the process around AI from scratch.
• Measure cost, time and quality before and after.
• Then scale.

Tools are cheap now. Redesign is the scarce skill.

POST
India made 24.51 billion UPI payments in August. The more interesting number is ₹1,217.

The August 2026 total was worth ₹29.82 lakh crore. Volume grew 22% year on year.

But the average UPI payment fell to about ₹1,217, from roughly ₹1,263 in July.

More payments. Smaller payments.

One month proves nothing on its own. But the pattern fits what friction removal does to behaviour.

Friction used to act as a filter. You bundled purchases. You waited. You thought twice.

Remove the friction and three things follow:
• Purchases get smaller and more frequent.
• Impulse becomes the default.
• Businesses built on tiny, repeated payments become viable. Quick commerce is the obvious example.

My hypothesis: micro-subscriptions, pay-per-use AI tools and creator tipping are next in line in India.

For founders, the lesson: design for the ₹49 decision, not the ₹4,900 one.

For consumers, the lesson is an uncomfortable mirror. A thumbprint replaced the pause. Your budget now depends on friction you add back yourself.

The next big Indian consumer brands will win on frequency, not ticket size. Hypothesis, not a forecast.

POST
7 in 10 people now hesitate to trust someone with different values or information sources.

The finding comes from the 2026 Edelman Trust Barometer. Edelman calls the shift insularity. Grievance turned inward.

One more number from the same report: "my employer" is the most trusted institution, at 78% among employees. Business overall sits at 64%. Government at 53%.

Here's what this means for branding.

Trust no longer flows from institutions to people. Trust flows sideways, through people we already know and people who feel like "us".

A logo is an institution. A person is a peer.

Three implications:
• Founder-led and employee-led content will outperform brand-account content. My hypothesis, based on where trust sits.
• Consistency beats reach. A small audience who sees you as "one of us" is worth more than a big audience who sees you as an ad.

Personal branding used to be optional for founders and professionals.

In 2026 the media found a candidate. Medvi, a GLP-1 telehealth company started from a Los Angeles home with $20,000 and more than a dozen AI tools. $401 million in sales in its first full year. Tracking toward $1.8 billion in 2026, per reports.

Allegations, not verdicts. But the lesson doesn't need a verdict.

AI multiplies whatever you point AI at. Ad creative. Customer support. Operations. Also shortcuts.

A tiny team removes the people who used to say "we shouldn't run this ad". Compliance. Legal. A sceptical senior marketer.

Those roles slow you down. Those roles also keep you alive.

The one-person unicorn is real. The one-person risk team is the part nobody pitches.

If you're building lean with AI, budget for judgment. A fractional compliance advisor costs less than a regulator's attention.

POST
Developers believed AI made them 20% faster. The stopwatch said 19% slower.

In 2025, METR ran a randomized trial with 16 experienced open-source developers on 246 real tasks in their own large codebases.

Before starting, the developers expected AI to speed them up by 24%.

Afterwards, they estimated AI had sped them up by 20%.

Measured result: tasks took 19% longer with AI allowed.

Compare GitHub's 2023 experiment, where developers with Copilot finished a fresh, self-contained coding task 55.8% faster.

Both studies are real. The difference is the task. A clean new task versus a messy, mature codebase you know better than any model.

In February 2026, METR said developers are likely more sped up now than in early 2025. METR also said selection effects make the new data weak evidence, because developers who didn't want to work without AI declined to join.

The lesson I take has nothing to do with coding.

How productive AI makes you feel is a poor measure of how productive AI makes you.

AI removes the effort you notice: typing, drafting.

AI adds effort you don't notice: reviewing, correcting, re-prompting, waiting.

So measure. Pick one recurring task. Time the task with and without AI for two weeks. Decide with data, not vibes.
